Academic Coaching

Support that builds capability, not dependence.

I work with students at all levels who need more than someone to explain the next question — including those navigating learning disabilities, executive-function challenges, academic setbacks, or simply a way of learning that doesn’t fit neatly into the standard approach.

Together, we figure out where the process is breaking down and build the strategies, systems, and confidence to move forward.

01Method

The student is the system.

There is no perfect planner, study method, explanation, or colour-coded calendar waiting to save everyone. Good support begins by understanding the individual learner: how they process information, what gets in the way, what already works, and what they’re actually trying to accomplish.

01 / Understand

Find the actual point of friction.

“I’m bad at science” can have a dozen different meanings. Maybe the concepts aren’t clicking. Maybe the student understands them but can’t retrieve them on a test. Maybe they have plenty of ideas for an essay but no idea how to organize them. Or maybe getting started takes longer than doing the work itself.

Before trying to fix the problem, we figure out what the problem actually is.

02 / Design

Build around the learner.

Once we know where the friction is, we can build around it. That might mean breaking a large assignment into concrete steps, redesigning a study routine, creating a system for tracking deadlines, finding a different way into a difficult concept, preparing for a conversation with a teacher, or turning “I have no idea where to start” into something actionable.

03 / Transfer

Make the strategy portable.

A system that only works while I’m sitting beside you isn’t a very good system. I explain why we’re trying a strategy, involve students in adjusting it, and gradually hand more of the process back to them. The aim is for students to leave with approaches they can recognize, adapt, and use when the next unfamiliar problem shows up.

02Support

Flexible help for complex academic lives.

Academic strategy & executive function

Planning assignments, managing deadlines, studying effectively, initiating tasks, organizing materials, preparing for tests, and recovering when the plan inevitably goes sideways.

Subject support

Concept review and problem-solving across academic subjects, with explanations adapted to how the student learns rather than repeated louder in increasingly desperate ways.

Writing & communication

Turning scattered ideas into structured writing, developing and revising assignments, interpreting expectations, planning presentations, and figuring out what you actually want to say and how to say it clearly.

Confidence & self-advocacy

Helping students understand what works for them, recognize where they need support, ask useful questions, make use of accommodations, and take an increasingly active role in their own education.
